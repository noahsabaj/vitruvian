## World-model search — Dreamer 4 out, TD-MPC2 rejected, LeWM in

First attempt: `nicklashansen/dreamer4`, the unofficial PyTorch port
of Dreamer 4 (Hafner 2025-09 arXiv 2509.24527). Hardware spec read
disqualifies us by two orders of magnitude:

> a machine with >256 GB RAM and 8 GPUs (>24 GB of memory each)

We have one 4060 Ti × 8 GB and 31 GB RAM. Dreamer 4 is out until/unless
we rent cloud compute.

Second attempt: `nicklashansen/tdmpc2` (Hansen et al., October 2023,
arXiv 2310.16828). Hardware-appropriate — 8 GB VRAM is their stated
single-task online-RL target. Was the parallel track in [ADR 003
addendum](decisions/003-graduated-vitruvian.md). User explicitly
rejected: "TD-MPC2 is 3 years old. I absolutely do not want to use a
three year old world model. Deploy agents to research and find a
modern alternative."

Dispatched two research agents (general-purpose) to sweep Q4 2025 —
April 2026 for world-model methods that (a) are newer than TD-MPC2,
(b) target continuous control, (c) have public code, (d) fit single-
GPU single-task. Condensed finding table:

| Method | Released | Humanoid | 8 GB fit | Paradigm |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| LeWM | Mar 2026 | No (PushT, reacher) | ✓ (15 M params) | JEPA WM + CEM planning, offline |
| Newt | Nov 2025 | ✓ DMControl humanoid | ✓ | TD-MPC2 descendant, language-conditioned |
| WIMLE | Feb 2026 | ✓ 8/14 HumanoidBench | ✓ | Uncertainty-aware WM |
| TD-M(PC)² | Feb 2025 | ✓ 61-DoF humanoid | ✓ | TD-MPC2 + policy constraint |
| Puppeteer | May 2024 | ✓ CMU humanoid | ✗ (≥24 GB) | TD-MPC2 hierarchical |
| BeyondMimic | Aug 2025 | Real Unitree G1 | likely | Diffusion motion prior + RL |

User picked LeWM: "I love JEPA, I love LeCun." Decision recorded in
[ADR 007](decisions/007-lewm-world-model.md). Thesis alignment is
strong — JEPA as the plastic world model on top of frozen
evolutionary-equivalent priors is literally what [ADR 003](decisions/003-graduated-vitruvian.md)
proposed. Risk acknowledged: LeWM has never been applied to humanoid
locomotion, so the G1 adaptation is genuine research, weeks not hours.

## Smoke test — validated on our hardware

First smoke attempt crashed with **CUDA OOM at batch=128** during the
ViT encoder attention layer (LeWM's default batch size). Our 8 GB
card has ~5.4 GB usable after desktop reserves ~3 GB.

Second attempt used `PYTORCH_CUDA_ALLOC_CONF=expandable_segments:True`
and `loader.batch_size=32`. Process ran cleanly at 100% GPU for 40+
minutes — but our shell pipeline (`... | grep ... | tail -20`)
buffered every stdout line until process exit, making progress
invisible. Killed.

Third attempt: `limit_train_batches=30 limit_val_batches=5` for a
fast-and-visible smoke, with `tee` piping stdout to a log file.
Initial Hydra override syntax failed (`trainer.limit_train_batches`
isn't in the default trainer dict; needed `++trainer.limit_train_batches=30`
to insert a new key). Fourth attempt worked end-to-end:

    Epoch 0/0  ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━  30/30  0:00:04 • 6.60 it/s
    Trainer.fit stopped: max_epochs=1 reached.

- **Wall:** ~1 minute total (most of it Lightning setup + data cache).
- **Throughput:** 6.60 iterations/s at batch 32 = 211 samples/s.
- **Validation:** `validate/loss_epoch` dropped 1.580 → 1.345 across
  the two checkpoints (sanity + end-of-epoch). `pred_loss` 0.147 →
  0.079. World model is learning.
- **VRAM after CPU offload:** 0.23 GB allocated, 3.65 GB reserved —
  comfortable headroom at batch 32. Could likely push to 48 or 64
  if we want faster throughput.

Exit 0. Submodule, venv, dataset, and training loop all validated on
our hardware.

## M4.2 executed in same session — G1 expert dataset collected

`scripts/m4_collect_g1_rollouts.py` ships with an orchestrator-worker
design. First single-process attempt at 100 episodes × 200 steps hit
the same cumulative-Warp-VRAM leak we saw in M0.4 and M1-full:

    [worker] ep 20/100 ... rate= 77.5 steps/s ETA 202s
    RuntimeError: Failed to allocate 524288 bytes on device 'cuda:0'

Rewrote the collector to chunk into 15-episode subprocesses — same
subprocess-isolation pattern as M0.4's bench sweep. Each worker
collects its chunk, writes a temp HDF5, exits cleanly; the orchestrator
concatenates into a single output and deletes chunks. The 7th chunk is
a short 10-episode tail (100 = 6×15 + 10).

Final collection: **100 episodes, 19,690 steps total, 407.9 s wall
(~6.8 min), 607.4 MB HDF5.** Episode lengths 68–200 (3 early
terminations out of 100 — the PPO walker falls under some
joystick-command combinations it wasn't trained to handle, which is
actually useful signal for the world model). `swm inspect` confirms:

    Columns
    action  (19690, 29)           float32
    pixels  (19690, 224, 224, 3)  uint8
    proprio (19690, 103)          float32
    state   (19690, 71)           float32

Throughput improved steadily across chunks (69 → 71 → 79 → 70 → 70 →
70 → 72 steps/s) as Warp's on-disk kernel cache warmed up.

LeWM's own `stable_worldmodel.data.HDF5Dataset` loads the merged file
cleanly: shapes, dtypes, ep_len, ep_offset all correct.

**Hydra config written** at
`external/le-wm/config/train/data/g1.yaml` — local submodule edit
(marked as dirty; we don't push into upstream). `frameskip: 1` since
G1 control is already at 50 Hz, tighter than tworoom/pusht's 20 Hz
effective rate.

## Next session — M4.3: first LeWM training on G1 data

The LeWM pipeline works on our hardware. The research arc now begins.
First concrete next step:

1. Write a rollout collector that loads the M1 PPO walker (checkpoint
   at `checkpoints/m1-g1-full/000043253760`), runs N episodes on
   `G1JoystickFlatTerrain` with random joystick commands, and writes
   trajectories into the HDF5 format `stable_worldmodel.data.HDF5Dataset`
   expects: keys `pixels` (head-cam frames), `action` (29-D),
   `proprio` (103-D state), `state` (optional raw qpos/qvel),
   plus `ep_len` and `ep_offset` index arrays.
2. Target dataset size: O(50k steps) for a first attempt, matching
   LeWM's PushT scale. That's ~1,000 episodes at ~50 steps each, or
   ~20 min of sim time.
3. Use subprocess isolation pattern (see M0.4 / M1 journal entries)
   so the rollout collector doesn't blow up VRAM.

Downstream steps (later sessions):

- Register a G1 env wrapper under `stable_worldmodel.envs` so LeWM's
  eval path can instantiate it.
- Write a G1-specific Hydra `config/train/data/g1.yaml` and
  `config/eval/g1.yaml`.
- Train the LeWM JEPA on G1 rollouts (expected: hours of wall clock
  on our 4060 Ti).
- Evaluate via CEM planning for joystick-conditioned walking.
- Iterate.

## Watch items carried forward

- Default LeWM batch size is 128; on our 8 GB we need ≤ 32. Fine for
  now but may limit scale when we extend to G1 pixel-plus-proprio
  observations — world-model capacity may need reduction to
  `encoder_scale: tiny` (already default) or `patch_size: 16`.
- LeWM's `validate/sigreg_loss` is a regularizer term on representation
  variance (the "sig" in LeJEPA = "sigma regularization"). Default
  weight produces values around 14-16 on our smoke run. Worth
  understanding before tuning.
- Dataset disk budget: pusht 50 GB + tworoom 12 GB + potential G1
  rollouts (estimate ~10-30 GB for 50k steps at 240p) will push us
  toward tight disk. Monitor.
- DINOv3 HF access is granted; swap `scripts/m3_dinov3_latents.py`
  to use `AutoModel.from_pretrained` sometime when we're cleaning up.
